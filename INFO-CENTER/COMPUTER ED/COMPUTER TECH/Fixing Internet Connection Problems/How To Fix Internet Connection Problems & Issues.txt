SECTION 1: INTRODUCTION & HOW TO USE THIS GUIDE


Welcome! This guide will help you diagnose and fix virtually any internet 
connection problem you encounter. Whether you're experiencing complete loss of 
internet, slow speeds, or intermittent disconnections, you'll find solutions here.

HOW TO USE THIS GUIDE:
- Start with Section 2 to identify your specific problem type
- Follow the troubleshooting steps in order (they progress from simplest to most complex)
- Read carefully and follow each step completely before moving to the next
- Keep notes of what you try and the results
- If you reach a point where you need to contact your ISP, you'll have useful 
  information to provide them

IMPORTANT NOTES:
- Do NOT skip steps - many issues are resolved by basic troubleshooting
- Some steps require a restart - make sure to save your work first
- When unplugging equipment, wait the FULL time specified
- If a step doesn't work, simply move to the next one


SECTION 2: QUICK DIAGNOSIS - IDENTIFY YOUR PROBLEM TYPE


Before you start troubleshooting, identify which category describes your problem:

PROBLEM TYPE A: COMPLETE LOSS OF INTERNET
Symptoms:
  • No devices can connect to the internet
  • Modem shows no internet signal
  • Router has no internet access
  • All lights are off or showing errors

ACTION: Go to SECTION 3 (Basic Troubleshooting)



PROBLEM TYPE B: INTERMITTENT CONNECTION
Symptoms:
  • Internet drops randomly throughout the day
  • Connection works sometimes but not always
  • Frequent disconnects and reconnects
  • Connection drops during specific activities

ACTION: Go to SECTION 4 (Intermediate Troubleshooting)



PROBLEM TYPE C: SLOW INTERNET SPEED
Symptoms:
  • Pages load slowly
  • Videos buffer frequently
  • Downloads take longer than expected
  • Speed is much slower than your plan

ACTION: Go to SECTION 4 (Intermediate Troubleshooting) + Run Speed Tests



PROBLEM TYPE D: DEVICE-SPECIFIC ISSUES
Symptoms:
  • Only certain devices can't connect
  • WiFi works on some devices but not others
  • Device is connected but has no internet access
  • Only specific applications have problems

ACTION: Go to SECTION 6 (Device-Specific Fixes)



PROBLEM TYPE E: WIFI-ONLY ISSUES
Symptoms:
  • WiFi shows as available but won't connect
  • WiFi is very slow compared to wired connection
  • WiFi drops frequently
  • Can't find your network

ACTION: Go to SECTION 7 (WiFi-Specific Issues)



PROBLEM TYPE F: ETHERNET-ONLY ISSUES
Symptoms:
  • Wired connection not working
  • Ethernet cable connected but no internet
  • "Cable unplugged" error message

ACTION: Go to SECTION 8 (Ethernet-Specific Issues)


SECTION 3: BASIC TROUBLESHOOTING (START HERE!)


STEP 1: CHECK FOR SERVICE OUTAGES


Why: If your ISP is experiencing an outage, troubleshooting won't help. You 
need to know if the problem is on your end or theirs.

How to Check:
1. Use a smartphone with mobile data (not WiFi)
2. Visit your internet provider's website
3. Look for a "Status Page" or "Outage Map"
4. Check if there are reported issues in your area
5. Alternatively, visit ThousandEyes.com or DownDetector.com
6. Search for your ISP name to see current status

What to Do If There's an Outage:
- Wait for your ISP to resolve the issue
- There's nothing you can do on your end
- Keep checking the status page for updates
- Note: You may be entitled to service credits if outages are frequent

If No Outage Found: Continue to Step 2



STEP 2: POWER CYCLE YOUR EQUIPMENT (THE MOST IMPORTANT STEP!)


Why: Power cycling resets your equipment and often fixes connection problems 
instantly. This simple step resolves approximately 80% of internet issues.

IMPORTANT: Follow this exact sequence and timing:

STEP 2A: Power Down
1. Unplug the power cable from your MODEM (the device from your ISP)
2. Unplug the power cable from your ROUTER (the WiFi device)
3. If your equipment has backup batteries, remove them
4. Wait EXACTLY 60 seconds (count slowly: 1-1000, 2-1000... to 60-1000)
   This allows the equipment to fully discharge

STEP 2B: Power Up the Modem First
1. Plug the modem's power cable back in
2. Wait 2-3 minutes for the modem to fully start up
3. Look at the lights on the modem:
   - Power light should be solid green or white
   - Internet light should be solid green or white
   - If lights are flashing, wait another minute
4. Do NOT plug in the router yet

STEP 2C: Power Up the Router
1. After the modem is fully ready, plug in the router
2. Wait 2-3 minutes for the router to fully start up
3. Look at the lights on the router:
   - Power light should be solid
   - WiFi light should be solid or flashing (this is normal)
   - Internet/WAN light should be solid

STEP 2D: Test Your Connection
1. Try to open a website or send an email
2. If internet works, you're done! Problem solved!
3. If internet still doesn't work, continue to Step 3

Expected Result: 
✓ Internet connection restored and working normally



STEP 3: CHECK PHYSICAL CONNECTIONS


Why: Loose or damaged cables are one of the most common causes of internet 
problems. A simple visual inspection can reveal the issue.

STEP 3A: Check the Modem Connections
1. Look at the cable coming from the wall (the thick coaxial cable)
   - This is the connection from your ISP
   - Make sure it's hand-tight (don't over-tighten)
   - Look for any visible damage, kinks, or cuts
   - Clean the connector ends with a dry cloth

2. Check the power cable
   - Make sure it's firmly plugged into the modem
   - Make sure it's plugged into a working outlet
   - Test the outlet by plugging in a lamp to confirm it works

3. Check the cable from modem to router
   - This is a thin network cable (Ethernet cable, looks like a phone cord)
   - Make sure it's plugged into the modem's WAN or Internet port
   - Make sure the other end is plugged into the router's WAN or Internet port
   - The connection should click when fully seated

STEP 3B: Check the Router Connections
1. Check the power cable
   - Make sure it's firmly plugged in
   - Make sure it's plugged into a working outlet
   - Test the outlet by plugging in another device

2. Check Ethernet cables (if you have wired devices)
   - Make sure cables are plugged into LAN ports (not WAN)
   - Make sure cables are fully inserted and clicked in
   - Look for visible damage

3. Look for cable damage anywhere
   - Check for cuts, frays, or exposed wires
   - Look for kinks or severe bends
   - Check if cables are pinched under furniture
   - Look for signs of chewing (if you have pets)

STEP 3C: Inspect Cable Connectors
1. Look at the RJ45 connectors (the ends of network cables)
   - They should look clean and undamaged
   - All 8 pins should be visible
   - No bent or missing pins
   - No discoloration or corrosion

2. If you find damaged cables:
   - Replace them with new cables
   - Try the new cables before continuing
   - If new cables fix the problem, you're done!

Expected Result:
✓ All connections are secure and tight
✓ No visible damage to cables
✓ All lights should be on or blinking normally

If Still Not Working: Continue to Step 4



STEP 4: CHECK EQUIPMENT STATUS LIGHTS


Why: The lights on your modem and router tell you the status of your 
connection. Understanding them helps diagnose problems quickly.

STEP 4A: Modem Light Indicators

Typical modem lights include:
- POWER: Should be solid green/white
- INTERNET/WAN: Should be solid green/white (not red or off)
- DOWNLOAD/UPLOAD: May flash (normal)
- WiFi (if built-in): Should be on

What Each Status Means:

POWER Light:
  ✓ Solid Green/White = Modem has power (GOOD)
  ✗ Off = Modem not powered, check power cable
  ✗ Red = Error, restart the modem

INTERNET/WAN Light:
  ✓ Solid Green/White = Connected to ISP (GOOD)
  ✗ Flashing = Trying to connect, wait 2 minutes
  ✗ Red = Connection error, check ISP status or restart
  ✗ Off = Not connected, check cables from wall

DOWNLOAD/UPLOAD Lights:
  ✓ Flashing = Data transfer happening (NORMAL)
  ✓ Off when idle = Normal
  ✗ Constantly on = May indicate issue

ACTION BASED ON LIGHT STATUS:

If Internet light is RED or OFF:
  1. Check the coaxial cable from the wall
  2. Try restarting the modem (power cycle)
  3. If still red after restart, call your ISP

If Power light is OFF:
  1. Check power cable connections
  2. Try a different outlet
  3. Try a different power cable
  4. If still off, the modem may be broken

STEP 4B: Router Light Indicators

Typical router lights include:
- POWER: Should be solid
- INTERNET/WAN: Should be solid
- WiFi 2.4G: Should be solid or flashing
- WiFi 5G: Should be solid or flashing (if available)

What Each Status Means:

POWER Light:
  ✓ Solid Green/Blue/White = Router is on (GOOD)
  ✗ Off = Router not powered, check power cable
  ✗ Red = Error, restart the router

INTERNET/WAN Light:
  ✓ Solid Green/Blue/White = Connected to modem (GOOD)
  ✗ Flashing = Connecting, wait 1 minute
  ✗ Red = Not connected, check cable from modem
  ✗ Off = Not connected, check connections

WiFi Lights (2.4G and/or 5G):
  ✓ Solid or Flashing = WiFi is broadcasting (GOOD)
  ✗ Off = WiFi is disabled, may need to enable in settings

ACTION BASED ON LIGHT STATUS:

If Internet light is RED or OFF:
  1. Check the Ethernet cable between modem and router
  2. Make sure it's plugged into the WAN/Internet port
  3. Try a different Ethernet cable
  4. Restart the router

If Power light is OFF:
  1. Check power cable
  2. Try a different outlet
  3. Try a different power cable

If WiFi lights are OFF:
  1. Look for a WiFi button on the router
  2. Press and hold it for 3 seconds to turn WiFi on
  3. If no button, restart the router
  4. If still off, you may need to enable WiFi in router settings

Expected Result:
✓ All lights showing normal status (green/blue, not red)
✓ Internet light is solid, not flashing
✓ Power light is on

If Still Not Working: Continue to Section 4 (Intermediate Troubleshooting)


SECTION 4: INTERMEDIATE TROUBLESHOOTING


STEP 5: TEST WITH A WIRED CONNECTION (ETHERNET)


Why: Testing with an Ethernet cable helps you determine if the problem is with 
your WiFi or with your internet connection itself. This narrows down the cause.

How to Test:
1. Get an Ethernet cable (the thin network cable)
2. Plug one end into a LAN port on your router
   - LAN ports are usually numbered 1, 2, 3, 4
   - NOT the WAN/Internet port (usually different color)
3. Plug the other end into your computer's Ethernet port
4. Wait 10 seconds for the connection to establish
5. Try to open a website or send an email

Possible Results:

RESULT A: Wired connection works fine
  → The problem is with WiFi
  → Go to SECTION 7 (WiFi-Specific Issues)

RESULT B: Wired connection doesn't work either
  → The problem is with your internet connection
  → Continue to STEP 6

RESULT C: Wired connection is very slow
  → Go to STEP 8 (Speed Testing)



STEP 6: CHECK FOR LOOSE OR DAMAGED WIRES (DETAILED)


Why: Even if you checked this in Step 3, cables can be problematic in subtle 
ways. This is a more detailed inspection.

STEP 6A: Coaxial Cable (from wall to modem)
This is the thick cable, usually with metal ends.

Detailed Inspection:
1. Follow the cable from the wall to the modem
2. Look for any damage along the entire length:
   - Cuts or frays in the outer covering
   - Exposed copper wire inside
   - Cracks in the insulation
   - Kinks or sharp bends
   - Signs of being stepped on or crushed

3. Check the connectors:
   - The connector should thread smoothly onto the modem
   - Should feel snug and secure (hand-tight, not over-tight)
   - No missing or bent pieces on the connector

4. Test the connection:
   - Gently wiggle the cable while watching the modem lights
   - If lights flicker, the connection is loose
   - Tighten the connector slightly

5. If you suspect damage:
   - Try replacing the cable
   - Use a new coaxial cable from your ISP or a hardware store
   - Test again to see if the problem is fixed

STEP 6B: Network Cables (Ethernet)
These are thin cables that look like oversized phone cords.

Detailed Inspection:
1. Check each Ethernet cable:
   - Modem to router
   - Router to computer (if wired)
   - Any other wired connections

2. For each cable, inspect:
   - The entire length for cuts or damage
   - Both ends for bent pins or corrosion
   - The plastic clips (they should not be broken)

3. Test connections:
   - Unplug and replug each cable
   - You should hear a small click when fully inserted
   - If the cable doesn't click, it's not fully seated

4. Swap cables:
   - Try a different Ethernet cable in the same connection
   - If the new cable works, the old one was bad
   - Replace the damaged cable

STEP 6C: Power Cables
1. Check power cables for damage
2. Make sure connections are tight
3. Test outlets with another device
4. If an outlet doesn't work, try a different outlet
5. Never use damaged power cables

Expected Result:
✓ All cables are intact with no visible damage
✓ All connections are secure and click into place
✓ No loose connections

If Still Not Working: Continue to STEP 7



STEP 7: RESTART YOUR COMPUTER/DEVICE


Why: Your computer's network settings can sometimes become confused. A restart 
clears these issues and refreshes the connection.

How to Restart:
1. Save all your open work
2. Close all applications
3. Click the Start menu (Windows) or Apple menu (Mac)
4. Select "Restart" or "Shut Down"
5. Wait for the computer to fully shut down
6. Wait 30 seconds
7. Turn the computer back on
8. Wait for it to fully load (you'll see the login screen)
9. Log back in
10. Test the internet connection

Expected Result:
✓ Computer connects to internet
✓ Websites load normally
✓ Internet speed is normal

If Still Not Working: Continue to STEP 8



STEP 8: RUN A SPEED TEST


Why: A speed test tells you if you're getting the internet speed you're paying 
for. This helps identify if the problem is slow internet or something else.

How to Run a Speed Test:

Method 1: Ookla Speedtest (Most Popular)
1. Open your web browser
2. Go to Speedtest.net
3. Click the big blue "GO" button
4. Wait for the test to complete (about 1-2 minutes)
5. Note down these numbers:
   - Download Speed (Mbps)
   - Upload Speed (Mbps)
   - Ping (ms)

Method 2: Google Speed Test
1. Open Google.com
2. Type "speed test" in the search box
3. Click "Run Speed Test"
4. Wait for results

How to Interpret Results:

DOWNLOAD SPEED (Mbps):
- Compare to your internet plan
- You should get at least 80% of what you pay for
- Example: If you pay for 100 Mbps, you should get 80+ Mbps

UPLOAD SPEED (Mbps):
- Usually much lower than download
- Check your plan to see what you should expect

PING (ms):
- Should be below 50ms for normal use
- Below 20ms is excellent
- Above 100ms may cause lag

What the Results Mean:

✓ Speed matches your plan:
  → You're getting the speed you pay for
  → Problem may be with a specific application
  → Continue to SECTION 6 (Device-Specific Issues)

✗ Speed is much lower than your plan:
  → You're experiencing slow internet
  → Continue to STEP 9 (Optimize Your Connection)

✗ Speed is very low or test won't complete:
  → Possible connection problem
  → Continue to SECTION 5 (Advanced Troubleshooting)



STEP 9: OPTIMIZE YOUR CONNECTION


Why: Your WiFi signal strength, router placement, and interference from other 
devices can all affect internet speed.

STEP 9A: Check WiFi Signal Strength
1. Look at the WiFi icon on your device
2. Count the bars:
   - 4-5 bars = Excellent signal (GOOD)
   - 3 bars = Good signal (ACCEPTABLE)
   - 2 bars = Weak signal (POOR)
   - 1 bar = Very weak signal (VERY POOR)

If signal is weak:
1. Move closer to the router
2. Test speed again
3. If speed improves, your router placement is the problem

STEP 9B: Improve Router Placement
Move your router to:
- A central location in your home
- High up (not on the floor)
- Away from walls and metal objects
- Away from the microwave or cordless phone
- Away from large appliances

Move away from:
- Metal objects (filing cabinets, metal shelves)
- Microwaves (they emit WiFi-interfering signals)
- Cordless phones and baby monitors
- Thick walls and doors
- Water tanks or aquariums

STEP 9C: Reduce Interference
WiFi operates on 2.4 GHz and 5 GHz frequencies. Other devices use these:

Devices causing 2.4 GHz interference:
- Microwaves
- Cordless phones
- Baby monitors
- Bluetooth devices
- Old wireless devices

How to reduce interference:
1. Move interfering devices away from the router
2. Switch to 5 GHz WiFi (faster, less interference)
3. Change your WiFi channel in router settings
   (See SECTION 5 for advanced WiFi configuration)

STEP 9D: Check for Background Activity
Sometimes other devices or applications use bandwidth:

On your computer:
1. Open Task Manager (Windows) or Activity Monitor (Mac)
2. Look at Network or Network tab
3. Identify applications using network
4. Close unnecessary applications
5. Run speed test again

On your network:
1. Check if other people are streaming video or downloading large files
2. Ask them to pause their activities
3. Run speed test again

STEP 9E: Disable Power Saving Features
Some devices reduce WiFi performance to save power:

For Windows:
1. Open Settings
2. Go to Network & Internet > WiFi
3. Click Manage known networks
4. Select your WiFi network
5. Click Properties
6. Toggle "Metered connection" OFF

For Mac:
1. Open System Settings
2. Go to WiFi
3. Click Details
4. Check your settings

Expected Result:
✓ Speed test shows improvement
✓ Internet feels faster
✓ No lag or buffering

If Still Slow: Continue to SECTION 5 (Advanced Troubleshooting)


SECTION 5: ADVANCED TROUBLESHOOTING


STEP 10: CHECK YOUR IP ADDRESS AND NETWORK SETTINGS


Why: Your device needs a valid IP address to connect to the internet. 
If the IP address is invalid, you won't have internet access.

FOR WINDOWS:

Step 1: Open Command Prompt
1. Click Start menu
2. Type "cmd" or "Command Prompt"
3. Click "Command Prompt" in the results
4. A black window will open

Step 2: Run ipconfig command
1. Type: ipconfig /all
2. Press Enter
3. Look for information about your network adapter

Step 3: Check Your IP Address
Look for a section that says "Ethernet adapter" or "Wireless LAN adapter"

Find these values:
- IPv4 Address: Should look like 192.168.X.X or 10.X.X.X
- Subnet Mask: Should be 255.255.255.0
- Default Gateway: Should look like 192.168.1.1 or 10.0.0.1
- DHCP Enabled: Should say "Yes"

What the values mean:

✓ IPv4 Address starting with 192.168 or 10:
  → You have a valid private IP address (GOOD)

✗ IPv4 Address is 0.0.0.0:
  → Your device didn't get an IP address
  → Go to STEP 11 (Fix IP Address Issues)

✗ IPv4 Address is 169.254.X.X:
  → Your device couldn't reach the DHCP server
  → Go to STEP 11 (Fix IP Address Issues)

FOR MAC:

Step 1: Open Terminal
1. Click Spotlight (magnifying glass, top right)
2. Type "Terminal"
3. Click Terminal in the results

Step 2: Run ifconfig command
1. Type: ifconfig
2. Press Enter

Step 3: Check Your IP Address
Look for "inet" (not "inet6")

Find these values:
- inet: Should look like 192.168.X.X or 10.X.X.X
- netmask: Should show 0xffffff00
- broadcast: Should look like 192.168.X.255

✓ inet address starting with 192.168 or 10:
  → You have a valid IP address (GOOD)

✗ inet address is 169.254.X.X:
  → Your device couldn't reach the DHCP server
  → Go to STEP 11

Expected Result:
✓ You have a valid IP address
✓ IP address starts with 192.168 or 10
✓ DHCP is enabled

If Still Not Working: Continue to STEP 11



STEP 11: FIX IP ADDRESS ISSUES


Why: If your device has an invalid IP address (0.0.0.0 or 169.254.X.X), it 
can't connect to the internet. Releasing and renewing the IP address often fixes this.

FOR WINDOWS:

Step 1: Open Command Prompt (as Administrator)
1. Click Start menu
2. Type "cmd"
3. Right-click "Command Prompt"
4. Click "Run as Administrator"
5. Click "Yes" if prompted

Step 2: Release Your IP Address
1. Type: ipconfig /release
2. Press Enter
3. Wait for the command to complete
4. You should see "Successfully released IP address"

Step 3: Renew Your IP Address
1. Type: ipconfig /renew
2. Press Enter
3. Wait for the command to complete
4. You should see "Successfully renewed IP address"
5. Note the new IPv4 address shown

Step 4: Verify the New IP Address
1. Type: ipconfig
2. Press Enter
3. Check that IPv4 Address is no longer 0.0.0.0 or 169.254.X.X
4. It should start with 192.168 or 10

Step 5: Test Your Connection
1. Close Command Prompt
2. Try to open a website
3. If it works, you're done!

FOR MAC:

Step 1: Open System Settings
1. Click Apple menu (top left)
2. Click "System Settings" or "System Preferences"

Step 2: Go to Network Settings
1. Click "Network" in the left menu
2. Select your connection (WiFi or Ethernet)
3. Click "Advanced"

Step 3: Renew Your IP Address
1. Click the "TCP/IP" tab
2. Click "Renew DHCP Lease" button
3. Wait for it to complete
4. Click "OK"

Step 4: Test Your Connection
1. Close System Settings
2. Try to open a website
3. If it works, you're done!

Expected Result:
✓ IPv4 Address is now valid (192.168.X.X or 10.X.X.X)
✓ Internet connection works
✓ Websites load normally

If Still Not Working: Continue to STEP 12



STEP 12: FLUSH YOUR DNS CACHE


Why: DNS is the service that translates website names (google.com) into IP 
addresses. If the DNS cache gets corrupted, websites won't load even though 
you have internet.

Symptoms of DNS problems:
- Websites won't load, but you have internet
- Some websites work, others don't
- You can't access websites by name, but can access by IP address
- Getting "Cannot find server" errors

FOR WINDOWS:

Step 1: Open Command Prompt (as Administrator)
1. Click Start menu
2. Type "cmd"
3. Right-click "Command Prompt"
4. Click "Run as Administrator"
5. Click "Yes" if prompted

Step 2: Flush DNS Cache
1. Type: ipconfig /flushdns
2. Press Enter
3. Wait for completion
4. You should see "Successfully flushed the DNS Resolver Cache"

Step 3: Test Your Connection
1. Close Command Prompt
2. Try to open a website
3. If websites load, you're done!

FOR MAC:

Step 1: Open Terminal
1. Click Spotlight (magnifying glass, top right)
2. Type "Terminal"
3. Click Terminal in the results

Step 2: Flush DNS Cache
The command varies by macOS version:

For macOS 10.15 and newer:
1. Type: sudo dscacheutil -flushcache
2. Press Enter
3. Type your password (you won't see it as you type)
4. Press Enter
5. Wait for completion

For older macOS:
1. Type: sudo killall -HUP mDNSResponder
2. Press Enter
3. Type your password
4. Press Enter

Step 3: Test Your Connection
1. Close Terminal
2. Try to open a website
3. If websites load, you're done!

Expected Result:
✓ DNS cache is flushed
✓ Websites load normally
✓ No more "Cannot find server" errors

If Still Not Working: Continue to STEP 13



STEP 13: CHANGE YOUR DNS SERVER

Why: Sometimes your ISP's DNS servers are slow or have issues. Using a public 
DNS server can improve performance and reliability.

Popular Public DNS Servers:
- Google DNS: 8.8.8.8 and 8.8.4.4
- Cloudflare DNS: 1.1.1.1 and 1.0.0.1
- Quad9 DNS: 9.9.9.9 and 149.112.112.112

FOR WINDOWS:

Step 1: Open Settings
1. Click Start menu
2. Click "Settings" (gear icon)
3. Click "Network & Internet"

Step 2: Go to Network Settings
1. Click "WiFi" or "Ethernet" (depending on your connection type)
2. Click "Manage known networks" or "Advanced options"
3. Scroll down and click "Edit" next to your network
4. Look for "DNS server assignment"
5. Click "Edit"

Step 3: Change DNS Server
1. Select "Manual"
2. Toggle "IPv4" ON
3. Delete any existing DNS addresses
4. Enter these two DNS addresses:
   - Primary: 8.8.8.8
   - Secondary: 8.8.4.4
5. Click "Save"

Step 4: Test Your Connection
1. Close Settings
2. Try to open a website
3. If websites load faster, you're done!

Alternative Method (via Router):
For a more permanent solution, change DNS in your router settings:

1. Open a web browser
2. Type: 192.168.1.1 or 192.168.0.1
3. Log in with your router username/password
   (Default is often admin/admin, check your router manual)
4. Look for "DNS Settings" or "DHCP Settings"
5. Change the DNS servers to 8.8.8.8 and 8.8.4.4
6. Save and restart the router

FOR MAC:

Step 1: Open System Settings
1. Click Apple menu
2. Click "System Settings" or "System Preferences"
3. Click "Network"

Step 2: Select Your Connection
1. Click "WiFi" or "Ethernet" (depending on your connection type)
2. Click "Advanced"

Step 3: Go to DNS Tab
1. Click the "DNS" tab
2. Look at "DNS Servers"

Step 4: Change DNS Server
1. Click the "+" button to add a new DNS server
2. Type: 8.8.8.8
3. Click the "+" button again
4. Type: 8.8.4.4
5. Click "OK"
6. Close System Settings

Step 5: Test Your Connection
1. Try to open a website
2. If websites load faster, you're done!

Expected Result:
✓ Websites load faster
✓ More reliable connections
✓ No more DNS timeout errors

If Still Not Working: Continue to STEP 14



STEP 14: SCAN FOR VIRUSES AND MALWARE

Why: Malware and viruses can consume bandwidth, block connections, or 
interfere with network settings.

Symptoms of malware:
- Internet is very slow
- Can't connect to certain websites
- Unexpected network activity
- Excessive data usage
- Computer is running slowly in general

How to Scan:

Step 1: Download an Antivirus Program
Recommended free options:
- Windows Defender (built-in to Windows)
- Malwarebytes (free version)
- Norton LifeLock (free trial)
- Bitdefender (free trial)

Step 2: Run a Full System Scan
For Windows Defender:
1. Click Start menu
2. Type "Windows Security"
3. Click "Windows Security"
4. Click "Virus & threat protection"
5. Click "Scan options"
6. Select "Full scan"
7. Click "Scan now"
8. Wait for scan to complete (may take 1+ hour)

For Malwarebytes:
1. Download from Malwarebytes.com
2. Install the program

STEP 24: CHANGE WIFI CHANNEL


Why: WiFi channels can have interference from neighboring networks. Changing 
to a less congested channel can improve performance.

UNDERSTANDING WIFI CHANNELS:

2.4 GHz WiFi (most common):
- Channels: 1, 6, 11 (non-overlapping channels)
- Many devices use this frequency
- More interference possible

5 GHz WiFi (faster, less congestion):
- Many more channels available
- Less interference
- Shorter range than 2.4 GHz

RECOMMENDED CHANNELS:
- 2.4 GHz: Channel 1, 6, or 11
- 5 GHz: Channel 36, 40, 44, 48, or higher (depends on your location)

How to Change Channel:

Step 1: Log Into Router
1. Open web browser
2. Go to 192.168.1.1 (or your router IP)
3. Log in with admin/password

Step 2: Find WiFi Settings
1. Look for "Wireless" or "WiFi" settings
2. Look for "2.4 GHz" or "5 GHz" section
3. Find "Channel" setting

Step 3: Select New Channel
For 2.4 GHz:
1. Try Channel 1, 6, or 11
2. Avoid channels 2-5, 7-10, 12-13 (overlapping)

For 5 GHz:
1. Try Channel 36, 40, 44, or 48
2. Or let router auto-select

Step 4: Save Changes
1. Click "Save" or "Apply"
2. Router may restart
3. Wait 1-2 minutes for changes to take effect

Step 5: Reconnect Devices
1. Your devices may disconnect temporarily
2. Reconnect to WiFi
3. Wait for connection to stabilize

Step 6: Test Speed
1. Wait 2 minutes after change
2. Run a speed test
3. If faster, keep the new channel
4. If slower, try a different channel

Expected Result:
✓ WiFi channel changed successfully
✓ Internet speed may improve
✓ Less interference and interference from neighboring networks

SECTION 8: ETHERNET-SPECIFIC ISSUES


PROBLEM: Wired Ethernet connection not working

Use this section if your WiFi works but your wired connection doesn't.

STEP 27: CHECK ETHERNET CABLE CONNECTION


Why: The most common cause of Ethernet problems is loose or incorrect 
connections.

Step 1: Check Cable Connections
1. Locate the Ethernet cable on your device
2. Verify it's plugged into the Ethernet port (not USB)
3. Verify the other end is plugged into a LAN port on the router
4. Make sure it's not plugged into the WAN/Internet port

Step 2: Verify Cable is Secure
1. Listen for a "click" sound when inserting the cable
2. Gently tug on the cable to make sure it's secure
3. Look for the connector clip - it should be in place

Step 3: Check Router Port
1. Make sure you're using a LAN port, not WAN
2. LAN ports are usually numbered 1, 2, 3, 4
3. WAN port is usually a different color or labeled differently
4. Try a different LAN port if the first one doesn't work

Step 4: Check for Link Light
1. Look at the Ethernet port on your device
2. There should be a small light next to the port
3. Green light = connection established (GOOD)
4. No light = no connection (check cable)
5. Orange/amber light = connection but slower speed

Expected Result:
✓ Ethernet cable is securely connected
✓ Cable is in correct port (LAN, not WAN)
✓ Link light is green

If Still Not Working: Continue to STEP 28
