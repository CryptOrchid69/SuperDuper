COMPLETE BEGINNER'S GUIDE TO FIXING PC PROBLEMS
ULTRA-DETAILED VERSION FOR ABSOLUTE BEGINNERS
BEFORE YOU START: LEARN THE BASICS
Understanding Your Computer Screen
When you turn on your computer, you will see a screen that looks like a desktop. This is called the DESKTOP.

THE TASKBAR (Bottom of Screen)
At the very bottom of your screen, you will see a horizontal bar. This is called the TASKBAR.

In Windows 11:

The taskbar is at the bottom
In the middle of the taskbar, you will see a Windows logo (looks like four squares)
This is the START BUTTON
To the right of the Start Button, you might see icons for programs (like a web browser)
On the far right, you will see the time and date
In Windows 10:

The taskbar is at the bottom
On the left side of the taskbar, you will see a Windows logo (four squares)
This is the START BUTTON
Next to it is a search icon (looks like a magnifying glass)
The rest of the taskbar shows program icons
On the far right, you will see the time and date
THE START BUTTON - WHERE IS IT?
For Windows 11:

Look at the very bottom of your screen
Look in the middle of the bottom bar
You will see a Windows logo (4 squares arranged in a 2x2 grid)
This is the START BUTTON
To click it, move your mouse cursor to it and click the left mouse button once
For Windows 10:

Look at the very bottom-left corner of your screen
You will see a Windows logo (4 squares)
This is the START BUTTON
To click it, move your mouse to it and click the left mouse button once
WHAT HAPPENS WHEN YOU CLICK THE START BUTTON?
When you click the Start Button, a menu will pop up showing:

A search box at the top
Various app icons and programs
A power button at the bottom (for shutdown/restart)
USING THE MOUSE
The mouse is the device in your hand that you use to control the cursor (arrow) on screen.

Moving the mouse: Move the mouse on your desk to move the arrow on the screen
Left-click: Press the left button on the mouse once
Right-click: Press the right button on the mouse once
Double-click: Press the left button twice quickly
USING THE KEYBOARD
The keyboard is the device with letters and numbers that you use to type.

Pressing a key: Push down a key and release it
Holding a key: Push down a key and keep it held down while doing something else
Typing: Press keys one at a time to enter text
SECTION 1: INTERNET & NETWORK CONNECTION PROBLEMS
Problem #1: No Internet Connection (Computer Says "No Internet")
What This Means: Your computer is connected to Wi-Fi or ethernet, but cannot access the internet.

QUICK FIX #1: Check If Wi-Fi Is Turned On
Step 1: Look at your screen

Look at the very bottom-right corner of your screen
You will see several small icons (this is called the system tray)
One of these icons should look like Wi-Fi signal bars (looks like curved lines) or a network cable icon
Step 2: Click the network icon

Move your mouse cursor to the Wi-Fi or network icon
Click it once with the left mouse button
A small menu will pop up showing available networks
Step 3: Check if Wi-Fi is on

In this menu, you should see your network name listed
Below the network name, it should say "Connected"
If it says something else, or if you don't see your network, continue to the next step
Step 4: Turn on Wi-Fi if it's off

In the network menu, look for a Wi-Fi toggle (a button that can be on or off)
If it's OFF (appears grayed out), click it once to turn it ON
Wait a few seconds
Step 5: Connect to your network

Look at the list of available networks
Find your network name (usually something like "Linksys" or your internet provider's name)
Click on it
A box will pop up asking for your Wi-Fi password
Type your password exactly (be careful with uppercase and lowercase letters)
Click "Connect" button
Wait 10-20 seconds
Try using the internet (open a web browser and go to a website)
QUICK FIX #2: Check Airplane Mode
Airplane Mode is a setting that turns off all wireless connections. If it's on, you can't use Wi-Fi.

Step 1: Open Settings

Look at the bottom-left or bottom-center of your screen
Find the Windows logo (4 squares) - this is the START BUTTON
Click it once with the left mouse button
A menu will pop up
Step 2: Click Settings

In the menu that appeared, look for a gear icon (⚙️)
This is the Settings app
Click it once with the left mouse button
A new window will open
Step 3: Find Network & Internet

In the Settings window, look at the left side
You will see a list of options
Find and click "Network & Internet"
The right side of the window will change to show network options
Step 4: Check Airplane Mode

On the right side, look for "Airplane Mode"
You will see a toggle button (it looks like a switch that can be ON or OFF)
If it shows ON (the switch is blue or highlighted), click it to turn it OFF
Wait a few seconds
Check if you can now access the internet
QUICK FIX #3: Restart Your Modem and Router
Your modem = The device provided by your internet company. It usually has blinking lights and sits in a cabinet or on a shelf.

Your router = The device that sends Wi-Fi signal. Sometimes it's built into the modem, sometimes it's separate.

Important: This will temporarily disconnect everyone using the Wi-Fi. Make sure to tell other people in your house.

Step 1: Find your modem and router

Look around your house for the device(s) with blinking lights
The modem usually has an internet cable connected to it
The router (if separate) usually has antennas sticking out
Step 2: Unplug the modem

Find the power cable connected to the modem
Follow it to the power outlet (the wall socket)
Gently pull the power plug out of the wall socket
The lights on the modem will stop blinking and turn off
Step 3: Wait 30 seconds

Count slowly: one-one-thousand, two-one-thousand... thirty-one-thousand
This gives the modem time to fully power down
Step 4: Plug the modem back in

Push the power plug back into the wall socket
The lights on the modem will start blinking again
Wait 2-3 minutes for all the lights to stop blinking (this means the modem is fully restarted)
Step 5: Unplug the router (if you have a separate one)

If you have a separate router (a different device with antennas), find its power cable
Gently pull the power plug out of the wall socket
The lights will turn off
Step 6: Wait 30 seconds

Count slowly again: one-one-thousand, two-one-thousand... thirty-one-thousand
Step 7: Plug the router back in

Push the power plug back into the wall socket
Wait 2-3 minutes for the lights to stop blinking
Step 8: Test your internet

Open a web browser (like Chrome, Firefox, or Edge)
Go to a website (like google.com)
See if it loads
QUICK FIX #4: Forget and Reconnect to Your Wi-Fi Network
Sometimes your computer "remembers" incorrect Wi-Fi information. Forgetting the network and reconnecting can fix this.

Step 1: Open Settings

Click the START BUTTON (Windows logo at bottom of screen)
Look for the gear icon (⚙️) in the menu
Click it to open Settings
Step 2: Go to Network & Internet

In the Settings window, look at the left side
Click "Network & Internet"
Step 3: Click Wi-Fi

On the left side, you should see "Wi-Fi"
Click it
Step 4: Click Manage Known Networks

On the right side, you will see several options
Look for "Manage known networks" or "Manage Wi-Fi networks"
Click it
Step 5: Find your network

You will see a list of networks you've connected to before
Find your network name in the list
Click on it to select it
Step 6: Forget the network

A button should appear that says "Forget"
Click the "Forget" button
Your computer will forget this network
Step 7: Reconnect to the network

Go back to the network menu
Click the Wi-Fi icon in the bottom-right corner of your screen again
Find your network name in the list
Click on it
Step 8: Enter your password

A box will pop up asking for your Wi-Fi password
Type your password exactly (uppercase and lowercase matter)
Click "Next" or "Connect"
Wait 10-20 seconds
Try using the internet
QUICK FIX #5: Reset Network Settings (Advanced - Only if Above Doesn't Work)
⚠️ WARNING: This is more technical. Only try if the above fixes didn't work.

What you're about to do: You will use the Command Prompt to send special commands to Windows to reset your network settings.

Step 1: Open Command Prompt as Administrator

Click the START BUTTON (Windows logo at bottom)
A menu will pop up
Type the words: "command prompt" (use your keyboard to type these letters)
You will see "Command Prompt" appear in the search results
Right-click on "Command Prompt" (click with the right mouse button)
A smaller menu will pop up
Click "Run as administrator"
A box might appear asking "Do you want to allow this app to make changes?" - Click "Yes"
Step 2: Type the first command

You will now see a black window with white text (this is Command Prompt)
At the bottom, you will see a blinking cursor
Type exactly: netsh winsock reset
Press the Enter key on your keyboard
Wait for it to say "OK" or finish processing
Step 3: Type the second command

Type exactly: netsh int ip reset
Press the Enter key
Wait for it to finish
Step 4: Type the third command

Type exactly: ipconfig /release
Press the Enter key
Wait for it to finish
Step 5: Type the fourth command

Type exactly: ipconfig /renew
Press the Enter key
Wait for it to finish
Step 6: Type the fifth command

Type exactly: ipconfig /flushdns
Press the Enter key
Wait for it to finish
Step 7: Close Command Prompt

Click the X button in the top-right corner of the Command Prompt window
The window will close
Step 8: Restart your computer

Click the START BUTTON
Click the Power icon (looks like a circle with a line at the top)
Click "Restart"
Your computer will restart
Wait for it to fully boot up
Test your internet connection
QUICK FIX #6: Update Network Drivers
A driver is software that helps Windows talk to your Wi-Fi card. Old drivers can cause problems.

Step 1: Open Device Manager

Click the START BUTTON
Type: "device manager"
Click on "Device Manager" when it appears
Step 2: Find Network Adapters

In the Device Manager window, you will see a list of hardware devices
Look for "Network adapters" in the list
Click the arrow next to it to expand it
You will see your network adapter(s) listed below
Step 3: Update the driver

Right-click on your network adapter (usually says something like "Intel Wireless" or "Ethernet")
A menu will pop up
Click "Update driver"
Step 4: Let Windows search for updates

A new window will appear asking how to search for drivers
Click "Search automatically for updated driver software"
Windows will search for updates
This may take a few minutes
Step 5: Restart your computer

After the update completes, close all windows
Click the START BUTTON
Click the Power icon
Click "Restart"
Test your internet connection
Problem #2: Internet Is Very Slow
QUICK FIX #1: Move Closer to Your Router
The further you are from the router, the weaker the signal.

Find your router (the device with antennas sending Wi-Fi)
Move your computer closer to it
Remove any obstacles between your computer and the router (walls, metal objects, large furniture)
Test your internet speed again
Go to www.speedtest.net in your web browser to test your speed
QUICK FIX #2: Check How Many Devices Are Using Your Wi-Fi
Too many devices using Wi-Fi slows down your connection.

Look at other devices in your house (phones, tablets, other computers)
Turn off Wi-Fi on devices you're not actively using
If someone is downloading large files or watching videos, ask them to pause
Test your internet speed again
QUICK FIX #3: Restart Your Router
Follow the same steps as QUICK FIX #3 above (unplug for 30 seconds, plug back in)
Wait for lights to stop blinking
Test your internet speed
QUICK FIX #4: Try a Different Wi-Fi Network
Many routers broadcast two networks: 2.4 GHz (slower, longer range) and 5 GHz (faster, shorter range)

Click the Wi-Fi icon in the bottom-right corner
Look at your available networks
If you see two networks with similar names (one might say "5G" at the end), try the one you're NOT currently using
Click on the other network
Enter your password
Test your speed
QUICK FIX #5: Position Your Router Better
Make sure your router is not in a closet or enclosed space
Place it higher up (on a shelf, not on the floor)
Keep it away from metal objects (filing cabinets, metal shelves)
Keep it away from microwaves and cordless phones (they interfere with Wi-Fi)
Keep it in an open area for better signal
Test your speed again
SECTION 2: CHANGING YOUR PUBLIC IP ADDRESS
What Is a Public IP Address?
Think of your IP address like your home address. Every device on the internet has one. Websites can see your IP address when you visit them.

A public IP address looks like this: 192.168.1.1

METHOD 1: Restart Your Modem (Easiest Method)
This is the easiest way. It works for most people with home internet.

Step 1: Find your modem

Look for the device with blinking lights that your internet cable connects to
This is your modem
Step 2: Unplug the power cable

Find the power cable connected to the modem
Follow it to the wall socket
Gently pull the plug out of the wall socket
All lights on the modem will turn off
Step 3: Wait 5 minutes

Do not plug it back in yet
Wait for 5 full minutes (count: 60 seconds × 5)
This is important - it gives the modem time to completely reset
Step 4: Plug it back in

Push the power plug back into the wall socket
The lights will turn back on
Wait 2-3 minutes for all the lights to stop blinking
This means the modem has fully restarted
Step 5: Check your new IP address

Open your web browser (Chrome, Firefox, Edge)
Go to: www.whatismyipaddress.com
You will see your public IP address displayed on the screen
Write it down so you can compare it to what it was before
If the numbers are different, your IP address has changed!
METHOD 2: Release and Renew IP Address From Windows
This is an alternative method that sometimes works.

Step 1: Open Command Prompt

Click the START BUTTON
Type: "command prompt"
Right-click on "Command Prompt"
Click "Run as administrator"
Click "Yes" if asked for permission
Step 2: Release your current IP address

In the Command Prompt window, type exactly: ipconfig /release
Press the Enter key
Wait for it to complete (you might see some text appear)
Step 3: Wait 10 seconds

Don't do anything for 10 seconds
Just wait
Step 4: Renew your IP address

Type exactly: ipconfig /renew
Press the Enter key
Wait for it to complete
Step 5: Close Command Prompt

Click the X button in the top-right corner
The window will close
Step 6: Check your new IP address

Open your web browser
Go to: www.whatismyipaddress.com
See if your IP address is different
METHOD 3: Use a VPN (Virtual Private Network)
A VPN makes websites think you're in a different location with a different IP address.

Step 1: Choose a VPN

There are many VPN services available
Popular ones: NordVPN, ExpressVPN, ProtonVPN, Surfshark
Free option: ProtonVPN has a free version
Paid options: Usually cost $5-15 per month
Step 2: Download the VPN

Open your web browser
Search for the VPN name (for example: "nordvpn.com")
Go to their website
Click the "Download" button
A file will download to your computer
Step 3: Install the VPN

Find the downloaded file (usually in your "Downloads" folder)
Double-click on it to open it
Follow the installation instructions
Click "Install" when asked
Wait for the installation to complete
Click "Finish"
Step 4: Open the VPN

Click the START BUTTON
Look for the VPN app name in your programs
Click on it to open it
You might need to create an account (sign up)
Step 5: Connect to a VPN server

In the VPN app, look for a "Connect" button or a list of countries
Click "Connect" or select a country
The app will connect you to a VPN server
Wait a few seconds for the connection to establish
Step 6: Check your new IP address

Open your web browser
Go to: www.whatismyipaddress.com
Your IP address should now be different (it will show the location of the VPN server you connected to)
METHOD 4: Contact Your Internet Service Provider
If you need a permanent new IP address, you can ask your internet company to give you one.

Step 1: Find your ISP's phone number

Look at your internet bill
Find the phone number for your internet service provider
Or search online for "[Your ISP name] customer service"
Step 2: Call them

Call the phone number
Wait for a customer service representative
Tell them: "I would like you to assign me a new public IP address"
They will usually do this for you right away
Step 3: Restart your modem

The representative might tell you to restart your modem
Follow the steps from METHOD 1 above
Step 4: Check your new IP address

Go to www.whatismyipaddress.com
Your IP should be different
SECTION 3: PRINTER PROBLEMS
Problem #1: Printer Won't Print
QUICK FIX #1: Restart Your Printer
Step 1: Turn off your printer

Look at your printer
Find the Power button (usually on the front or side)
Press it to turn the printer OFF
The lights will turn off
Step 2: Unplug the power cable

Find the power cable connected to the printer
Follow it to the wall socket
Gently pull the power plug out of the wall socket
Step 3: Wait 30 seconds

Count slowly: one-one-thousand, two-one-thousand... thirty-one-thousand
Step 4: Plug it back in

Push the power plug back into the wall socket
Step 5: Turn on your printer

Press the Power button to turn the printer back ON
Wait for it to fully start up (lights will stop blinking)
Step 6: Try printing

Open a document or photo on your computer
Click File menu
Click Print
Click the Print button
See if the printer prints
QUICK FIX #2: Check Physical Connections
For Wired (USB) Printers:

Step 1: Check the USB cable

Find the USB cable that connects your printer to your computer
Follow it from the printer to the computer
Make sure both ends are firmly plugged in
If it's loose, gently push it in until it clicks
Step 2: Try a different USB port

Unplug the USB cable from your computer
Look at your computer for other USB ports (they look like small rectangular holes)
Most computers have several USB ports
Plug the USB cable into a different port
Wait 10 seconds
Try printing again
For Wireless Printers:

Step 1: Make sure the printer is on

Look at your printer
Make sure it's powered on (lights should be on or blinking)
If off, press the Power button
Step 2: Check if the printer's Wi-Fi is on

Look at your printer for a Wi-Fi button (usually shows Wi-Fi symbol)
Press it if it's available
Look for a Wi-Fi light on the printer
If it's on, it should be lit up (usually blue or green)
If it's off, press the Wi-Fi button to turn it on
Step 3: Make sure your computer and printer are on the same Wi-Fi network

Click the Wi-Fi icon in the bottom-right corner of your screen
Look at your network name
On your printer, look at its display screen or documentation
Make sure the printer is connected to the same network as your computer
If not, use the printer's buttons or screen to select the correct network
QUICK FIX #3: Check for Paper Jams and Low Ink
Step 1: Open all covers and panels

Look at your printer
Open every door, cover, and panel you can see
Look inside for any stuck paper
If you see paper, gently pull it out (be careful not to tear it)
Step 2: Check ink or toner levels

Look at your printer
There should be an ink or toner cartridge visible
Check if the ink level is low
If low, you will need to replace the cartridge with a new one
(Ask a family member or friend if you need help with this)
Step 3: Check paper tray

Look at the paper tray (where you load paper)
Make sure there is paper loaded in the tray
If empty, add paper (usually standard 8.5" x 11" white paper)
Step 4: Close all panels

Close every door and panel you opened
Make sure everything clicks shut
Step 5: Try printing again

Open a document
Click File > Print
Click Print button
See if it prints
QUICK FIX #4: Remove and Reinstall Your Printer
This tells Windows to "forget" about your printer and then set it up again fresh.

Step 1: Open Settings

Click the START BUTTON
Click the gear icon (⚙️) to open Settings
Step 2: Go to Bluetooth & Devices

In the Settings window, look at the left side
Click "Bluetooth & devices" or "Devices"
Step 3: Click Printers & Scanners

On the left side, you should see "Printers & scanners"
Click on it
Step 4: Find your printer

On the right side, you will see a list of printers
Look for your printer in the list
Click on it to select it
Step 5: Remove the printer

A "Remove" button should appear
Click the "Remove" button
Click "Yes" to confirm
Your printer is now removed from Windows
Step 6: Turn off your printer

Find your printer
Press the Power button to turn it OFF
Wait 10 seconds
Step 7: Turn on your printer

Press the Power button to turn it back ON
Wait for it to fully start up
Step 8: Add the printer back

Go back to the Printers & scanners settings (same steps as above)
Click the "Add device" button (or "Refresh" button)
Windows will search for printers
Wait 30 seconds for it to find your printer
When your printer appears in the list, click on it
Click "Add device" button
Windows will install your printer
Wait for it to complete
Step 9: Try printing

Open a document
Click File > Print
Make sure your printer is selected
Click Print
See if it works

SECTION 4: USB DEVICE NOT RECOGNIZED
Problem: USB Device Shows "Not Recognized" Error
This means Windows can't identify your USB device (flash drive, external hard drive, printer, etc.)

QUICK FIX #1: Try a Different USB Port
Step 1: Unplug your USB device

Find the USB cable connected to your device
Gently pull it out of the USB port
The USB device will be unplugged
Step 2: Wait 5 seconds

Just wait and do nothing for 5 seconds
Step 3: Try a different USB port

Look at your computer
Most computers have multiple USB ports (small rectangular holes)
Find a different USB port than the one you just unplugged from
Plug your device into this new port
Step 4: Check if it's recognized

Look at your screen
Windows might show a notification that the device is recognized
Try using your device (for example, open File Explorer to see the files)
QUICK FIX #2: Restart Your Computer
Sometimes a restart fixes USB recognition problems.

Step 1: Close all programs

Close any open documents or programs
Make sure nothing is being copied to or from the USB device
Step 2: Click Start Button

Click the START BUTTON (Windows logo)
Step 3: Click Power

Look for the Power icon in the menu
Click it
Step 4: Click Restart

Click "Restart"
Your computer will restart
Step 5: Wait for restart

Your computer will turn off and back on
Wait for Windows to fully load
This usually takes 1-2 minutes
Step 6: Plug in your USB device

After your computer is fully restarted
Plug your USB device back in
See if it's recognized
QUICK FIX #3: Check for Physical Damage
Step 1: Inspect the USB connector

Look at the metal connector part of your USB device
The metal part should look shiny and smooth
Check for:
Bent pins (small metal pieces that stick out)
Corrosion (green or white discoloration)
Dirt or debris (dust or particles)
Step 2: Clean the connector

Get a soft, dry cloth (like a microfiber cloth)
Gently wipe the metal connector with the cloth
Do this several times
Make sure it's clean and dry
Step 3: Plug it back in

Insert the USB device back into the USB port
Make sure it's fully inserted
Check if it's recognized
QUICK FIX #4: Update USB Drivers
Step 1: Open Device Manager

Click on "Device Manager" when it appears
Step 2: Find USB controllers

You will see several items listed
Step 3: Look for problem devices

Look at the items under "Universal Serial Bus controllers"
Some might have a yellow exclamation mark (!) next to them
These are the ones with problems
Right-click on any device with a yellow mark
Step 4: Update the driver

A menu will pop up
Click "Update driver"
Step 5: Let Windows search for updates

A new window will appear
Click "Search automatically for updated driver software"
Windows will search and install updates
This may take a few minutes
Step 6: Restart your computer

Close all windows
Click START BUTTON
Click Power icon
Click "Restart"
After restart, try your USB device again
QUICK FIX #5: Uninstall and Reinstall USB Drivers
If updating didn't work, try completely removing and reinstalling the driver.

Step 1: Open Device Manager

Click START BUTTON
Type: "device manager"
Click "Device Manager"
Step 2: Find USB Root Hub

Find "Universal Serial Bus controllers"
Click the arrow to expand it
Look for "USB Root Hub"
Step 3: Uninstall the driver

Step 5: Restart your computer

Windows will ask you to restart
Click "Restart"

Step 6: Windows will reinstall drivers

When your computer restarts, Windows will automatically find and install USB drivers
This may take a few minutes
Let it complete without interrupting
Step 7: Try your USB device

After the restart is complete
Plug in your USB device
See if it's recognized
QUICK FIX #6: Disable USB Selective Suspend

Step 1: Open Power Settings

Click the arrow next to it to expand it

Step 7: Click Apply and OK

Click the "Apply" button
Click the "OK" button
Close all windows
Step 8: Restart your computer

Click START BUTTON
Click Power icon
Click "Restart"
After restart, try your USB device
SECTION 5: SLOW COMPUTER PERFORMANCE
Problem #1: Computer Is Running Very Slowly
QUICK FIX #1: Restart Your Computer
This fixes most slowness problems!

Step 1: Save your work

If you have any documents or files open, save them first
Click File > Save or press Ctrl + S
Close all open programs
Step 2: Click Start Button

Click the START BUTTON (Windows logo at bottom)
Step 3: Click Power

Look for the Power icon (looks like a circle with a line at the top)
Click it
Step 4: Click Restart

A menu will pop up with options
Click "Restart"
Your computer will shut down and restart
Step 5: Wait for restart

Your screen will go black
Windows will start loading
You might see the Windows logo
This takes about 1-2 minutes
Step 6: Sign in

After the restart, you might need to sign in
Enter your password if prompted
Wait for your desktop to fully load
Step 7: Check speed

Try opening a program or website
See if things are faster now
QUICK FIX #2: Close Unnecessary Programs
Too many programs running at once slows down your computer.

Step 1: Open Task Manager

Look at the bottom of your screen (the taskbar)
Right-click on an empty area of the taskbar (not on any icons)
A menu will pop up
Step 2: Click Task Manager

In the menu, click "Task Manager"
A window will open showing running programs
Step 3: Look at the Processes tab

At the top of the Task Manager window, you should see tabs
Click on the "Processes" tab (if not already selected)
Step 4: Look at the list of programs

You will see a list of programs and how much memory each is using
Look at the "Memory" column to see which programs use the most
Step 5: Close unnecessary programs

Look for programs you don't need right now
Common programs to close:
Chrome, Firefox, Edge (web browsers)
Spotify, YouTube (music/video apps)
